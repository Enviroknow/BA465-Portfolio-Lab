Portfolio Decision Lab

Construct an illustrative portfolio, apply economic scenarios, and evaluate whether your allocation matches an investor's mandate. Use Part C of your Week 4 Investment Analysis Workbook to record and interpret your results.

1. Choose an investment mandate

Investment mandates explain whom a portfolio serves and establish objectives and constraints. These are hypothetical teaching policies, not APU's actual investment policy.

2. Allocate $100,000

Enter the five portfolio weights as percentages in increments of 5. The weights must add to 100%. Each equity category represents a hypothetical basket, not a particular company.

Equities respond differently to company growth and the economy. Bonds and cash also carry risks. This simplified exercise does not calculate historical volatility or statistical correlations.

3. Test market conditions

Choose one fictional one-year economic scenario. Returns are illustrative, not forecasts or actual historical observations.

Portfolio return = sum of (asset weight × asset return). Contributions are in percentage points. No trading costs, fees, taxes, deposits, or withdrawals are included.

4. Revise and compare

First, record your initial portfolio and recession results in Part C of your workbook. Change at least two allocations, keeping your weights at 100% and within the growth-endowment's illustrative limits. Retest the same recession scenario. Then load an income-oriented or reserve mandate and compare.

Finally, test another market scenario. Did the change improve one outcome at the expense of another? Is a portfolio appropriate because of its highest observed return, or because it aligns with the mandate?

Interpretation: Performing better in one fictional scenario does not establish that a portfolio is the best choice. Evaluate tradeoffs and the investor's purpose.
